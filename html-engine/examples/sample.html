The Cartographer's Brief

How a small survey craft maps an uncharted region in a single pass, and what the choices behind that pass teach about working under hard constraints.

Survey Office · Prepared for the Standing Committee
What we'll cover

Three questions, in order

  • The approach — why one clean pass beats many partial sweeps
  • The instruments — what each sensor contributes, and where they overlap
  • The trade-off — what a single-pass map gives up, stated plainly
The claim

A map drawn in one pass is honest about its own seams.

Repeated sweeps hide their gaps in the overlap. A single pass shows you exactly where the certainty ends — and that edge is the most useful line on the chart.

The shape of the work

What the pass gains

  • A single coordinate frame across the whole region
  • No seams from stitching separate surveys together
  • A clear, honest boundary where resolution falls off

What it costs

  • The outer band is a sketch, not a survey
  • Drift accumulates the further from the known edge
  • Fine detail waits for a second, targeted cycle
